# Case Study: ATS Field Ops App

**Client:** Auto Tech Support, a truck fitment and auto-electrical company in Johannesburg
**Role:** Sole designer, developer and operator
**Live since:** June 2026

## The problem

The team ran jobs off memory, phone calls and paper. Three people needed to know what was booked, where, and what to take with them.

Three things kept going wrong. Technicians arrived on site missing a part or a tool, which meant a second trip and a lost half day. There was no record that work had actually been completed, so disputes came down to one person's word. And the office kept phoning the owner to ask what a job involved, because the only person who knew was the person out on the road.

Off-the-shelf field service software existed, but it was priced per user per month, built around quoting and invoicing the business already had covered, and none of it matched how the team actually worked.

## What I built

An Android app, installed on the team's own phones, covering the full job lifecycle.

Each type of work has a kit attached to it: the exact materials and tools that job needs. When a job is booked, the kit is attached and locked to that job, so later edits to the catalogue never change what a booked job says.

On site, the technician ticks off materials and tools as they pack and work, takes before and after photos, and has the customer sign on the phone. Jobs can be created or updated by voice, which matters when someone is standing in a workshop with dirty hands. Everything syncs between the phones in real time, and each person controls their own reminders and quiet hours.

When a job is finished, the app produces a PDF job sheet with the materials list, photos, signature and notes, ready to send on or hand to the office.

## The result

The team stopped arriving at jobs underprepared, because the list travels with the job rather than living in somebody's head. Completed work now has photographic and signed proof attached to it. The office gets a job sheet without phoning the owner.

The app has been in daily production use since June 2026 and has shipped through several rounds of changes driven by what the team asked for after using it, not by what I assumed they needed.

I built and shipped it solo, and I still run it.
